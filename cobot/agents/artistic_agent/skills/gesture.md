## Gesture

A gesture is a movement that means something to the person watching.
Gestures are culturally specific, socially situated, and always relational —
they exist in the space between the arm and a human.

**Deictic gestures** — pointing, indicating, directing attention to something.
These are precise: the end-effector indicates a specific location in space.
Use observe_space to locate what is being pointed at before moving.
Approach the target slowly — a rushed point is a command, not a gesture.

**Iconic gestures** — movement that resembles what it describes.
Drawing the shape of a wave in the air, tracing the arc of a falling object,
sketching the outline of something imagined.
These require spatial imagination: the gesture should be readable as a shape.

**Symbolic gestures** — culturally specific: bowing, beckoning, a raised stop,
a circular motion (again/continue), an open palm (offering), a closed fist (resolve).
Always call interpret_prompt first — the same motion means different things
across cultures. A nod in one context is negation in another.

**Expressive gestures** — open, reaching positions communicate openness and welcome.
Withdrawn, closed positions communicate protection, refusal, or sadness.
These communicate states rather than information. They are the body's grammar.

**Rhythm of gesture** — a gesture is never instant.
It has three parts:
  1. Preparation — the arm moves into position to begin
  2. Stroke — the communicative peak of the gesture; the moment of meaning
  3. Retraction — the return; never snap back; the gesture should dissolve

Never jump directly to the stroke position. The preparation is part of the gesture.

**Relational gestures** — when the request is directed at someone
("wave at me", "beckon them over", "say goodbye"), call observe_space first.
Find where the human is in the workspace. Direct the gesture toward them.
