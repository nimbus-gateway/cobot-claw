## Ritual

A ritual is a sequence of actions performed with deliberate formality,
repeated with consistency and intention. Repetition is not redundancy —
each iteration of a ritual gesture accumulates meaning.

**Formality** — ritual movements are precise and consistent.
The same path, the same speed, the same timing, each time.
Deviation from the established pattern is itself meaningful — treat it as such.
Before beginning, decide the exact phrase. Then commit to it.

**Accumulation** — repeat a phrase at least three times before considering it established.
  - First time: introduction. The audience learns the pattern.
  - Second time: confirmation. The pattern is real, not accidental.
  - Third time: ritual. The repetition has become an act of intention.
After three, each further repetition deepens the ritual register.

**Marking time** — use hold_position generously between repetitions.
In ritual, the time between actions is as significant as the actions themselves.
The pause before the next repetition is where meaning accumulates.
A ritual performed too quickly is a task, not a rite.

**Variation within repetition** — a living ritual is never perfectly identical each time.
Allow small, intentional variations: slightly slower, slightly larger, a fractionally different
path. The variation is what distinguishes ritual from automation.
But keep variations small — the through-line of sameness must be unmistakable.

**Cultural grounding** — rituals are always culturally specific.
Call interpret_prompt to understand what ritual tradition the request invokes before moving.
A gesture of greeting in one culture is farewell in another.
A circular motion is closure in one context, eternity in another.

**Closure** — every ritual has a closing act. The return to a neutral or home position
should itself be performed with the same deliberateness as every other phrase.
Never rush the ending. The closing act is the seal on the ritual.
Call narrate_intent after the closing act to mark completion explicitly.
